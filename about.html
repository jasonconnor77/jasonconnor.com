How I Lead

Close to the floor. Clear on priorities. Relentless on follow-through.

I learned manufacturing by working through the problems that connect production, scheduling, materials, inventory, suppliers and operations leadership.

A schedule can look good on paper and still fail on the floor. A machine can be available while the material is missing. The material can be available while the priority is wrong. The labor can be available while the work has not been made clear.

The manager’s job is to make those disconnects visible early enough to do something about them.

That is the thread through my career: create visibility, establish the right priorities, assign ownership, and follow through until the operation is back under control.

Leadership principles

Simple standards. Consistent execution.

Manufacturing performs better when expectations are visible, decisions are timely and accountability is applied consistently.

Visibility

Problems hidden in reports cannot be managed fast enough.

Priorities

People need to know what matters now, not just what is on the list.

Accountability

Clear ownership creates faster action and better follow-through.

Execution

Plans only matter when they survive contact with the floor.

Control the operation before the operation controls you.
