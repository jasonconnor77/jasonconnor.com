About

I learned manufacturing by working through the problems that connect every department.

Production. Scheduling. Materials. Inventory. Suppliers. Operations leadership. The experience matters because a plan can look perfect on paper and still fail on the floor.

Good manufacturing leadership starts with knowing what is actually happening.

I’ve spent more than 20 years in manufacturing, working across production, scheduling, materials, inventory, supplier management, and operations leadership. I did not build my career in one position or one department.

A production schedule can look good on paper and still fail on the floor. A machine may be available, but the material is not there. The material may be there, but the priority is wrong. An order may be late because of capacity, a supplier issue, an inventory error, or because nobody had a clear view of what needed attention first.

That experience shaped the way I lead today: stay close to the operation, make the real problem visible, establish clear priorities, remove obstacles, and follow through until the result changes.

I have always been comfortable stepping into difficult situations—past-due schedules, shortages, poor visibility, inventory problems, competing priorities, and processes that have become harder than they need to be.

I also build solutions when existing tools do not provide what the operation needs. That has led me to develop production, scheduling, inventory, material-shortage, and management tools using Excel, Google Sheets, Google Apps Script, and ERP data. I am not a software developer trying to understand manufacturing. I am a manufacturing leader who learned how to build better tools for problems I was already responsible for solving.

01

Visibility before reaction

Problems are easier to solve when the operation can see them early and agree on what matters.

02

Priorities must be credible

A priority that ignores material, capacity, labor, or actual floor conditions is not an operating plan.

03

Accountability needs clarity

People perform better when expectations, status, ownership, and the definition of done are visible.

04

Tools should reduce friction

The best tool gives the right person the right answer without creating another process to manage.
